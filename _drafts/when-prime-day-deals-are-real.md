Prime Day is one of the biggest shopping events of the year. Your inbox fills up, every site has a "best Prime Day deals" list, and the Amazon home page turns into a wall of red sale tags.

Some of those deals are genuinely great. Some are prices you could have gotten on a random Tuesday. And some aren't deals at all.

The good news is you don't have to guess. Here's how I sort the real ones from the rest.

## Not every "Prime Day deal" is a Prime Day deal

During a big event like this, a lot of things get swept up in the excitement:

- **Regular sales get rebranded.** A discount that's been running for weeks suddenly has a Prime Day badge on it.
- **Third-party sellers join in.** Marketplace sellers set their own prices and their own "was" prices. Some are fair. Some aren't.
- **Other stores run competing sales.** That's actually good news for you, but it means the best price might not even be on Amazon.
- **Everybody publishes deal lists.** Many of them are built quickly and include anything with a discount label, real or not.

None of that means Prime Day is a scam. It just means the label alone doesn't tell you much.

## How to tell if a Prime Day deal is real

**1. Check the price history.** This is the big one. If the "deal" price matches what the item has sold for most of the past few months, it's not a deal. If it's at or near the lowest it's been, it's a real one.

**2. Ignore the "list price."** The crossed-out price is often a manufacturer's list price that almost nobody pays. Compare against what the item *usually* sells for, not what it says it's worth.

**3. Look at who's selling it.** "Ships from and sold by Amazon" is the cleanest comparison. If it's a third-party seller, check their ratings and make sure the item is new, not renewed or used, unless that's what you want.

**4. Compare other stores.** Other big retailers often run their own sales around the same time. A quick search can save you from paying more on Amazon for something that's cheaper elsewhere.

**5. Watch for the exact version.** Make sure the deal is on the color, size, storage or bundle you actually want. Sometimes only one variant is discounted.

## What tends to be worth watching

Based on how these events usually go, a few kinds of products are good to keep an eye on:

- **Amazon's own devices.** Amazon tends to discount its own products during its own events, like its streaming devices, smart speakers and e-readers. If you've been eyeing one, this is usually a good time.
- **Things you've already been tracking.** If you know an item's typical price, you'll spot a real drop instantly.
- **Everyday stuff you'll buy anyway.** Chargers, cables, storage, batteries. Small savings on things you'd buy regardless still count.

And a few things I'm more careful with:

- **Brands I've never heard of with huge discounts.** A giant percentage off a made-up "was" price isn't a deal.
- **Brand-new products.** The newest gear doesn't always get big discounts, and when it does, the price history is too short to tell you much.
- **Anything I didn't want before the sale started.** That's the impulse buy talking.

## Don't let the clock rush you

Prime Day comes with countdown timers, "Lightning Deals" and "claimed" progress bars. They're designed to make you feel like you'll miss out if you stop to think.

A real deal still makes sense after you check the price history. If the only reason to buy is a timer, let it go. There will be another sale, and many Prime Day prices show up again later in the year around other big shopping events.

## My Prime Day game plan

1. **Make a list before the event.** Write down what you actually need or have been waiting on.
2. **Look up typical prices ahead of time** so you know what a good price looks like.
3. **Set price alerts** on the items on your list.
4. **Check the deal against price history** before buying anything.
5. **Skip everything else.** If it wasn't on your list, it probably doesn't need to be in your cart.

## The bottom line

Prime Day deals can be real, and some of them are excellent. But the badge on the listing isn't proof. Price history, a quick comparison and a shopping list will tell you which deals are worth it.
