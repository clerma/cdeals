We've all done it. You decide you want a new pair of headphones, a laptop or a TV, and suddenly you want it *today*. You open a store app, add it to your cart, and check out at full price before you've even finished your coffee.

I've done it more times than I'd like to admit. And almost every time, I saw the same thing on sale a few weeks later for a lot less.

That's the hardest lesson in deal hunting: the best price almost never shows up the moment you want something. It shows up to the person who's willing to wait.

## Why waiting usually pays off

**Prices move all the time.** Tech prices aren't fixed. They change with sales events, new model launches, store competition and plain old inventory clean-outs. Something that's full price today can easily drop next week.

**New models push old ones down.** When a company announces a new version of a product, last year's model almost always gets cheaper. In a lot of cases the "old" one is nearly as good, at a much better price.

**Sales come around on a schedule.** Big shopping events happen every year: Black Friday and Cyber Monday, back-to-school season, major retailer sales events and holiday weekends. If you can wait for one of them, you'll usually save.

**Waiting kills impulse buys.** Give it a few days and you might find out you didn't really need it. That's the best deal of all: the $300 you never spent.

## How I practice patience (without forgetting about it)

Being patient doesn't mean doing nothing. It means having a plan.

1. **Know the price you want to pay.** Pick a number before you start shopping. When a deal hits it, you'll know it's time.
2. **Watch the price history.** Price trackers and browser extensions show you how a product's price has changed over time. If something "on sale" was cheaper last month, it isn't really a deal.
3. **Set alerts.** Let the deals come to you. Most price trackers and many stores let you get a notice when the price drops.
4. **Learn the product's cycle.** If a new version usually comes out in the fall, buying the current one in late summer at full price is probably a mistake.
5. **Keep a wish list.** Write down what you're waiting on. When a big sale hits, you'll be ready to move fast instead of browsing aimlessly.

## Watch out for fake urgency

Stores know impatience sells. That's why you see things like:

- Countdown timers
- "Only 2 left in stock!"
- "Deal ends tonight!"
- "Lowest price ever!" banners

Sometimes they're true. A lot of the time they're just there to rush you. A real deal will still make sense after you check the price history and sleep on it. If the only reason to buy is a ticking clock, slow down.

## When it's time to stop waiting

Patience is a tool, not a rule. There are times when waiting costs you more than it saves:

- **When you actually need it.** If your laptop died and you work from it, don't spend three weeks waiting to save $40.
- **When the price hits your target.** You set the number for a reason. When the deal shows up, take it.
- **When it's a real, verified low.** If the price history shows it's at or near the lowest it's ever been, waiting probably won't get you much more.
- **When stock really is limited.** Some deals, like clearance and open-box items, do sell out. If it's the right item at the right price, go for it.

The goal isn't to wait forever. It's to buy on your terms instead of the store's.

## The bottom line

The right deal is almost always coming. It just doesn't always show up the moment you want it to. A little patience, a target price and a few price alerts can save you real money on nearly everything you buy.
