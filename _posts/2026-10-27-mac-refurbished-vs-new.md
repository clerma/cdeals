Macs have a reputation for lasting a long time and holding their value. That's great when you own one. It's less great when you're trying to buy one, because Macs don't often get big discounts.

That's why refurbished Macs get so much attention. But is refurbished really the smarter buy, or should you just get a new one? Here's how I'd think it through.

## Not all "refurbished" Macs are the same

The word "refurbished" covers a lot of ground. Who did the work matters more than anything else.

**Apple Certified Refurbished.** This is Apple's own program. Macs are inspected and tested, come with the accessories you'd expect, and carry the same one-year limited warranty as a new Mac. They're also eligible for AppleCare, just like new. If you're buying refurbished, this is the safest place to start.

**Major retailer programs.** Big stores and marketplace programs sell refurbished Macs too, usually with their own return window or guarantee. Quality can be very good, but warranties and condition grades vary. Read the listing closely.

**Third-party sellers.** Some are excellent. Some aren't. Check seller ratings, the condition description, the warranty and the return policy before you buy.

## Why refurbished can make a lot of sense

- **You save money on the same machine.** A refurbished Mac from Apple's program is the same model you'd buy new, just at a lower price.
- **You may be able to afford a better configuration.** The savings can be the difference between the base model and the one with the memory or storage you really wanted.
- **Last year's model can be a sweet spot.** When a new Mac comes out, the previous generation often shows up refurbished, and it's usually still very capable.
- **Macs tend to last.** Since Macs often stay useful for years, a refurbished one can still give you a long run.

## Why new can be the better call

- **You want the latest model.** Brand-new models may not show up refurbished for a while.
- **You need a specific configuration.** Refurbished stock depends on what's available. If you need a particular color, chip, memory or storage combination, new gives you every option.
- **A sale closes the gap.** Retailers run sales on new Macs, and sometimes a new one on sale costs about the same as, or less than, a refurbished one. Always compare.
- **Education or work pricing.** If you qualify for discounts through school or work, the new price may be lower than you'd expect.

## The things to check either way

**Memory and storage.** On current Macs with Apple silicon, memory and storage are built in and can't be upgraded later. Whatever you buy is what you'll have for the life of the machine, so choose with the future in mind.

**The chip and model year.** Apple eventually stops supporting older Macs with new macOS updates. A newer model generally has more years of updates ahead of it. Check the model year, not just the price.

**Battery health.** For a MacBook, the battery is the part most affected by age and use. Apple's program addresses this, but with other sellers, ask about battery condition or look for a listing that mentions it.

**Warranty and return window.** You want time to test the Mac at home, plus coverage if something goes wrong later. Apple's program gives you the one-year warranty. Elsewhere, look for the longest coverage you can get.

**Activation Lock.** If you're buying from anyone other than Apple or a major retailer, make sure the previous owner removed it from their Apple Account. A locked Mac can be a very expensive paperweight.

## How I'd decide

1. **Pick the model and configuration you need,** especially memory and storage.
2. **Check the new price,** including any current sales.
3. **Check Apple Certified Refurbished** for the same configuration.
4. **Check major retailers** for refurbished and open-box options.
5. **Compare total value,** including warranty, return policy and AppleCare eligibility, not just the sticker price.

If refurbished saves meaningful money with the same warranty, it's an easy call. If the gap is small, new might be worth it.

## The bottom line

A refurbished Mac, especially from Apple's own program, can be one of the best ways to get a great computer for less. But new still makes sense when you need the latest model, a specific configuration, or when a sale makes the difference small. Compare carefully and you can't go too wrong.
